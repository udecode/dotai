# Verify App

Collect evidence for the selected claims. Task owns authorized repairs. An audit inspects and records findings; it does not silently authorize product edits. Explicit plan-only stops before driving. Reuse the caller's goal and plan, and never close its broader objective from a verification subtask.

1. Freeze the promised scope and stable acceptance IDs from current authority. Distinguish operation, provider-interface, copy-owner and scene denominators. Do not infer completeness from whatever routes the replacement happens to expose.
2. Discover the actual app-specific verification skill, runtime command, inventory and fixture owner. Use it when present. Missing tooling is a named capability gap; do not invent a doctor or browser command.
3. Bind evidence to source revision/candidate, serving environment, actor/permissions, fixture, URL/material state, viewport and artifact. Health or a successful build is a prerequisite, not proof of authentication or behavior.
4. Map required action, saved transition, read-back, downstream consequence and final artifact before driving. Capture each fact once and link it to every criterion it actually proves.
5. Observe the real entrypoint and states using available sanctioned browser/native/CLI/provider tools. Retain exact failures and unavailable states. An iframe count, screenshot of a menu or synthetic fixture does not prove a complete operation.
6. Record stable findings with observed failure, source owner, affected acceptance and proof needed to close. Recheck corrected, invalidated or previously uncovered rows only. An unrelated revision does not invalidate every capture; uncertain bindings stay unverified.

Read [profiles](references/profiles.md) for the selected verification mode. Use the existing plan/report with one row per promised operation: expected behavior, observed result, evidence, binding and passed/failed/blocked/not-run status. Keep explicit exclusions separate from unvisited work.

Audit completion and product success are different. Report their actual counts. Missing access leaves dependent coverage incomplete; failed product behavior remains failed even when the audit report is finished. `walkthrough` and `atlas` reuse final evidence rather than adding a second driving session.
